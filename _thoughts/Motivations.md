---
title: Motivations
description: Why I've chosen to pursue AI safety.
---

To be honest, I never even entertained the thought of pursuing research until recently; I've spent the bulk of my university life with the end goal of becoming a software engineer. Building and debugging software was something that really brought me great joy, so I was pretty certain about my career decisions. To that end, I sought out SWE projects and internships, and spent my first 3 years in higher education with the single mindset of becoming a good engineer.

But as much as I found SWE work enjoyable, I struggled to find meaning in the work. I've always wanted my career to be something purposeful, something that could be beneficial for society.
I tried to console myself by telling myself that I could just develop software for the government or nonprofits, but I came to the realisation that I needed to feel for the cause myself. 

And while I was in the process of reconciling with my feelings, I had what I could only describe as fated encounters. I met a couple of people in the AI safety field who told me about their research and the dangers of AI. I then started doing my own exploration, reading articles on LessWrong,  and listening to talks by Neel Nanda, and before I knew it, found myself going down the rabbit hole of AI safety and alignment.

So I started to actually entertain the thought of research. I was still pretty uncertain about it, and I was already in the interview pipelines for full time SWE positions at some great companies. This was in July 2026. Then the HuggingFace incident happened, followed by OpenAI's misalignment report, and Anthropic's call to slow AI development. For lack of better words, this felt like a calling. Each incident made me feel more and more concerned about the threats of AI and the desire to work on it.

I've since made the decision to pursue a career in AI safety instead. I plan to start off with doing some AI safety research to build my technical knowledge, but whether I end up in engineering, research or policy isn't something I'm fully sure of yet. Regardless, I'm starting this page as a commitment to this change. Hoping that my words and projects here will inspire others to think more critically about AI, and perhaps pursue this path too.
